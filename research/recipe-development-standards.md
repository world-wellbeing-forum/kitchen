# Recipe development and health evidence

This cookbook is a culturally grounded cooking resource, not a treatment for high LDL cholesterol, prediabetes or diabetes. A recipe cannot guarantee a lower LDL-C or A1C. Individual glucose response, medication, total carbohydrate intake, portion size, weight, activity and medical history matter.

## Editorial nutrition principles

1. **LDL-conscious cooking:** build meals around vegetables, legumes, soy foods, whole grains and fibre. Use measured unsaturated oils (such as canola, soybean or olive oil) in place of butter, ghee, cream, coconut oil and palm oil. Avoid describing a single dish as cholesterol-lowering. The 2026 ACC/AHA dyslipidemia guideline recommends dietary patterns rich in fruits, vegetables, legumes, whole grains and fibre, replacing saturated and trans fats with unsaturated fats.
2. **Blood-glucose-aware portions:** use the ADA plate as a flexible visual guide: about half a 9-inch plate non-starchy vegetables, one-quarter protein, one-quarter quality carbohydrate. Dal, peas, beans, potato, taro, rice, roti and millet all contribute carbohydrate; legumes can contribute both protein and carbohydrate. Avoid calling these foods carb-free or assigning a universal glycemic response.
3. **Protein-forward vegetarian meals:** use tofu, soy chunks/granules, beans, lentils, peas, low-fat paneer or plain low-fat dairy as appropriate. State vegan substitutions explicitly. Protein estimates are approximate and should be recalculated using ingredient weights and brand labels.
4. **No nuts, no eggs:** recipes are designed without nuts or eggs. Check packaged spice blends, sauces, dairy alternatives and cross-contact statements where allergy safety is relevant. Seeds are optional and must be labelled; do not assume seed tolerance.
5. **Cooking methods:** stovetop is the primary tested-in-development method. Microwave, oven and air-fryer directions must be specific to the dish, use appropriate cookware, include stirring/turning and doneness checks, and remain labelled as untested until kitchen-validated.
6. **Images and sources:** each recipe needs its own dish-specific image with documented ownership/licence and attribution where required. Never use a random image endpoint as if it were a verified photograph of the recipe. Cite the source of culinary inspiration and distinguish it from our adaptation.

## Disclaimer

Nutrition values on recipe pages are estimates, not laboratory analyses or individualized medical advice. Values vary with brands, ingredient size, cooking yield and serving size. People with diabetes or prediabetes should follow their own clinician or registered dietitian's carbohydrate and medication plan. People with kidney disease or other conditions may need different protein, potassium, phosphorus or sodium targets. Seek individualized advice from a registered dietitian nutritionist. This site does not diagnose, prevent or treat disease.
